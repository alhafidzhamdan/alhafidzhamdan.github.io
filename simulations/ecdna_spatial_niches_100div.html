A tumour section, two niches

A bird's-eye view of a tumour slice. Every cell in the section carries the ecDNA (one copy to start). Most of the tissue is background — a neutral microenvironment where ecDNA just drifts. Embedded in it are two spatially separate niches: with Selection in niche A = 0 all three regions drift alike (the neutral baseline). Raise it and cells inside niche A gain a local fitness advantage ∝ copy number (hypoxia, therapy, a growth-factor pocket), while niche B and the background stay neutral. A localised ecDNA amplification hotspot lights up at niche A while the rest of the section drifts toward loss.

Reading it: at selection 0 all three regions drift together — the neutral baseline everywhere. Turn up selection in niche A and it splits off: niche A climbs into a focal amplification while niche B and the background keep drifting to zero. The result is a spatially localised ecDNA hotspot — the same circle, different fate by location — heterogeneity written by the microenvironment.

Model · a lattice of cells inside the tissue; two ragged-edged niches sit in a neutral background, their membership graded (cells join a niche with probability = distance-based influence, so margins fade instead of a hard edge). Each division every site replicates (k→2k, cap 200) then Binomial(2k, ½) partition; the site is repopulated by a daughter drawn from its own region weighted by fitness w = 1 + s·k (s = the niche-A slider inside niche A, else 0). Positions are fixed, so the section evolves in place. Scrub to move through divisions.
