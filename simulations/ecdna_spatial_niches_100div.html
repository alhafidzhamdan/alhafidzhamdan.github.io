Extrachromosomal DNA · spatial selection

The same ecDNA, two niches

An ecDNA-bearing clone seeds two tumour niches, A and B, that start identical (every cell one copy). Each niche is its own population. With Selection in niche A = 0 both niches drift neutrally — ecDNA is a martingale that many lineages lose, so the two niches look alike. Raise it and niche A gains a local fitness advantage proportional to copy number (a selective microenvironment — hypoxia, therapy, a growth-factor gradient), while niche B stays neutral. Watch spatial heterogeneity emerge: niche A amplifies and fixes the ecDNA, niche B drifts toward loss.

Reading it: at selection 0 both curves drift the same way — down toward loss — the neutral baseline in each niche. Turn up selection in niche A and the curves split: niche A climbs as high-copy cells out-proliferate, niche B keeps drifting to zero. The gap between them is ecDNA spatial heterogeneity written by the microenvironment, not by the circle itself.

Model · two independent fixed-size populations (Wright–Fisher). Each division: replicate (k→2k, cap 200) then Binomial(2k, ½) partition; niche A resamples with fitness w = 1 + s·k, niche B with w = 1 (neutral). Both start with every cell at one copy. Scrub the slider to move through divisions.
