Extrachromosomal DNA · spatial clonal expansion

One founder cell, two fates

A bird's-eye view of a tumour slice. The tissue is full of ordinary cells carrying no ecDNA. In two spatially separate niches, a single cell is born carrying one ecDNA circle — one founder in niche A, one in niche B. As cells divide, each ecDNA+ cell can colonise a neighbouring cell or die. Where the microenvironment gives ecDNA a fitness advantage (niche A), the founder's progeny propagate — a clone grows outward from the point of origin. Where it does not (niche B, neutral), the lone founder drifts and dies out. Same founding event, opposite fate — decided by location.

Clonal expansion vs extinction

ecDNA+ cells over 100 divisions · niche A vs niche B

Reading it: both niches start with exactly one ecDNA+ cell. Under selection the niche A founder's clone grows and spreads outward, filling the niche, while the niche B founder drifts to extinction — a single lineage with no advantage almost always dies. Set selection to 0 and niche A dies too: without positive selection, propagation fails everywhere. The same founding circle, opposite fate — decided by the microenvironment.

Model · a fixed lattice of cells; one ecDNA+ founder cell is seeded in each niche, the rest are host cells with no ecDNA. Each division, every ecDNA+ cell may die (rate reduced by local selection ∝ its copy number) or colonise a random neighbouring host cell, whose ecDNA is the parent's replicated circles partitioned Binomial(2k, ½) (cap 200). Niche A applies a graded fitness advantage — strongest at its core, fading to the margin — so its lineage is supercritical and expands; the neutral niche B (and background) are subcritical, so a lone founder goes extinct.
