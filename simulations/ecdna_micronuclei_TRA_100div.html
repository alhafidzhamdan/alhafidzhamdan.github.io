Extrachromosomal DNA · micronucleus origin

Born in a micronucleus

A lagging chromosome is trapped in a micronucleus, shatters (chromothripsis), and the fragments re-ligate into a single circular ecDNA — one that fuses two non-homologous loci: EGFR on chr7 and MYCN on chr2, stitched by an inter-chromosomal translocation (TRA). There is no excision scar here — the founding junction is the TRA itself. Under selection on the double-oncogene dosage, the hub amplifies across 100 divisions, and the TRA's read support climbs to its maximum at division 100.

Reading it: the ecDNA carries one fragment from chr7 (EGFR) and one from chr2 (MYCN), so both loci amplify together — the red major-allele track climbs at each locus while the minor allele stays at 1. The founder TRA arcs between the two chromosomes (the interchromosomal fusion that closes the circle); like any junction on the amplicon its read support tracks copy number, rising to a maximum at division 100. Unlike the episomal circle, there is no excision scar — micronuclear chromothripsis fuses non-homologous loci rather than looping a segment cleanly out of one place.
