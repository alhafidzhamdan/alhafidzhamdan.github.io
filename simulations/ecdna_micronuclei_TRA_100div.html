Extrachromosomal DNA · micronucleus origin

Born in a micronucleus

A lagging chromosome is trapped in a micronucleus, shatters (chromothripsis), and the fragments re-ligate into a single circular ecDNA — a chaotic mosaic that fuses two non-homologous loci, EGFR on chr7 and MYCN on chr2. Chromothripsis never yields a single clean junction: the circle carries many rearrangements at once — multiple TRAs plus intra-chromosomal DEL, DUP and inversions — and the hallmark oscillating copy number. There is no excision scar. Under selection on the double-oncogene dosage the hub amplifies across 100 divisions, and every junction's read support climbs to its maximum at division 100.

Reading it: the shattered pieces of chr7 (EGFR) and chr2 (MYCN) are stitched back in scrambled order, giving the tell-tale oscillating copy number — segments bounce between amplified levels and deletions. The circle is held together by many junctions born in the one catastrophe: three TRAs arcing between the chromosomes plus intra-chromosomal DUP, DEL and inversion junctions. Every one rides on the amplicon, so their read support all climbs to a maximum at division 100. This is the opposite of the episomal circle's single clean excision — and there is no excision scar.
