Extrachromosomal DNA · neutral population dynamics

One episome, a hundred divisions

A single EGFR segment is excised once — in the founder cell only — into one acentric ecDNA. There is no second birth: from then on each circle simply replicates once per division and its copies segregate at random (no centromere). We follow a fixed-size population forward 100 divisions under pure drift — no selection — and watch what neutrality does to a newborn oncogene amplicon.

The neutral verdict: with no fitness advantage, ecDNA is a martingale that keeps hitting its absorbing state — the carrier fraction drifts downward as lineage after lineage partitions to zero. Yet the same randomness lets a lucky few cells stack up copies, so the maximum copy number spikes even while the population sheds the amplicon. Amplification that sticks needs selection on top of this — the neutral run is the null it has to beat.
